## 3. Corrections the Sol brief needs before its citations are reused

### 3.1 Unfaithful capitulation is misattributed to ref [2]

Sol §1 writes: "A subsequent study found that a model's reasoning state could remain correct while its emitted answer changed … what the authors call **unfaithful capitulation**. ([arXiv][2])" — but ref [2] is 2602.13093, whose abstract does not contain the term. The correct source is **arXiv:2605.29087** ("We call this unfaithful capitulation (UC)"), which is absent from the Sol reference list entirely (the repo's own `validation-log.md` already cites it correctly). Fix: point the UC sentence at 2605.29087; keep 2602.13093 for the nine-model attack findings and CARG failure. Sol §12 repeats the same [2] citation for the confidence claim — that one is correct (CARG is in 2602.13093).

### 3.2 "The KDD paper" label for DRL is unverified

The arXiv page for 2602.09945 shows no Comments/journal-ref; the full text contains no KDD/SIGKDD mention (checked including footnotes/acknowledgments); a public OpenReview search returns zero notes; no accepted-papers list confirms it. KDD 2026 is Aug 9–13, 2026 (Jeju), and its ADS-track OpenReview groups exist but expose no public record for this paper. The "KDD paper shared by Ramayya" phrasing exists only in the meeting notes. **Cite it as "arXiv:2602.09945 (preprint)" and ask Krishnan or Rao directly whether it is accepted at KDD 2026** — do not print "KDD paper" in any deliverable until then.

### 3.3 Precision caveats on the pathology automation-bias study (2603.11821)

1. The task was **tumor-cell-percentage estimation**, not full diagnostic decision-making — say "pathology experts on a quantification task," not "diagnosis."
2. Time pressure did **not** increase the *frequency* of automation bias: "While time pressure did not increase the frequency of automation bias, it appeared to intensify its severity." The *anchoring* effect (LMM coefficient on AI advice) is what "increased under time pressure." Sol's sentence ("Anchoring effects became stronger under time pressure") is accurate; any paraphrase claiming more-frequent automation bias under time pressure would be contradicted by the abstract.

Also worth labeling wherever used: of the ten arXiv items, only **2303.12502 (Behavior Research Methods, 2025)** and **2603.11821 (accepted, MELBA)** are peer-reviewed/accepted; the other eight are preprints as of 2026-07-16 and should carry a "preprint" tag on slides, per the repo quarantine rule that an arXiv identifier is not peer-review confirmation.

Search basis: full enumeration of Ramayya Krishnan's and Yubo Li + Rema Padman's arXiv output through 2026-07-16, an arXiv metadata search for Padman+hallucination, and targeted searches on the DRL coauthor team.

**Finding 1 (high confidence): the hallucination-detection content is inside the DRL paper itself.** arXiv metadata search "Padman hallucination" returns **zero** papers — no member of the group has a hallucination-titled paper. But the DRL full text (2602.09945) builds an explicit hallucination detector for clinical agent reasoning: discrepancies are categorized as "Missing clinically relevant factors (v_miss), hallucinated or irrelevant factors (v_halluc), and incorrect or incomplete reasoning paths," where "v_halluc are nodes introduced by the agent that are unsupported or irrelevant in context," and the judge outputs "(ii) which nodes are hallucinated or irrelevant." The Zoom summary's "KDD paper … on clinical agent reasoning for insights on agent behavior and hallucination detection" is therefore fully explained by **one** paper; the "second paper" memory may be an artifact.
