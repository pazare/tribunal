## 1. Autonomy spectrum; who decides vs who acts (94-815 L3 s9–11)

On your spectrum: an **agentic workflow with multi-agent deliberation** — not an autonomous agent. Your two axes separate cleanly: **the panel decides** (sealed ballots, charter-scoped, bounded rounds produce a recommendation tuple), and **a human acts** — nothing executes without ratification; the clinical variant is `DECISION_SUPPORT_ONLY` and the clinician holds action authority, with a live intervention endpoint and a safety-seat veto on the panel side. In delegated-autonomy phase terms (L8 s5–6): the loop runs at phase 4–5, with a hard human gate at ratification.

## 2. Why five sealed agents instead of one agent with reflection (L3 s39–45; L8 s9/s12)

Two of your five criteria force it, one is under test:
- **Verification burden:** escalation review doesn't decompose into subtasks — it decomposes into adversarial perspectives. Sealed independent seats plus an on-record adversary make each claim cheaper to verify *before* the clinician sees it. A reflection loop critiques itself inside one failure domain.
- **Human oversight:** the clinician's oversight object is *material disagreement*. A single agent with reflection self-erases its dissent; the panel preserves it as a first-class artifact.
- **Failure decorrelation is the hypothesis, not the assumption:** E2 and the architecture-comparison arm run the single-agent, cost-matched comparator. If the panel doesn't beat it on evidence-responsiveness and cue-resistance, that result is reported plainly. "More agentic is not automatically better" is in our protocol's spirit already — we accepted your trade-off ledger: latency and cost paid for auditability and decorrelated failure.

## 10. Seeds, trials, CIs, noisy ties (95-820 L8 s18)
